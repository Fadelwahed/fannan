## The nine steps

### 1. Reconcile the brief and define design success

Read the current step from the brief. Freeze a source snapshot with stable brief line IDs, page/template inventory, audience, principal job, conversion or task goal, known constraints and decision status. Resolve repository, stack, design tool, roles and languages from settings. Write observable success criteria for each visitor task. If a brief, rules file, authoritative role or essential fact is missing, record the gap and its owner; do not substitute a guessed fact.

Maintain a discovery ledger for audience needs, current workflows, pain points and constraints. Record evidence source/date, assumptions, confidence, design consequence and risk. Use supplied or already-authorized research, support and analytics evidence. Assign material assumptions an owner and validation method in Step 5. Never present brief assertions or visual references as user research; preserve existing contact and payment approvals. The `design-research` skill keeps this ledger next to its reference findings, and Step 5 checks it (`discovery-evidence`).

Write one proposed visual thesis: “For [audience] doing [job], the design makes [brief-backed distinction] visible through [hierarchy/content behavior].” This is internal rationale, not website copy. Cite the exact brief lines for each clause. Specify two or three operational consequences, such as making the next action prominent or putting evidence beside a decision. A decorative adjective alone is not a point of view. Exit when the source hierarchy, scope and decision map are unambiguous enough for content work.

### 2. Visitor needs, content and evidence

Copy comes before layout. Take approved copy from the brief or the project's writer; preserve exact approved wording. Build the copy deck with a stable slot ID, screen/state, purpose, text, language/direction, character range, tone note, source lines and all fact references. Every claim, statistic, named customer, logo and quotation needs a confirmed project fact and any required consent. Source presence alone is not confirmation. Record who checked it, when and against which source revision. A testimonial also needs its exact wording, speaker and permission.

Use visible `[placeholder]` for a missing line, listing its slot, owner and required source. Never create a plausible headline, number or customer as temporary content. Placeholders may support an explicitly incomplete wireframe; they fail Step 9. Give the writer a text-fit request when a slot is too long; widen or reflow the layout first. Do not cut copy, ellipsize content or invent a shorter claim to fit a box. An intentional read-more clamp may show an excerpt only when a declared state makes the complete approved text reachable at every checked width; all other cutting or ellipsis fails.

Collect image briefs describing the communication job, required content, rights and alt-text intent. Identify what not to show. Exit with an inventory that accounts for every slot, source, missing item and consent requirement. Known blocking content gaps remain visible.

### 3. Information architecture

Map each page and section to a visitor task and an exact brief line. Each section brief names its purpose, content slots, evidence, CTA and destination, expected state changes and success check. Remove sections with no job. A structural decision, grid break, illustration or motion proposal gets the same trace, so the critic can reject anything without one.

Build a focused reference board of three real, inspected references: one useful for hierarchy, one for content density/type and one for the relevant interaction. These are comparison roles, not required vendors or aesthetic templates. Record URL, inspection date, local evidence where rights permit, what works, what would be wrong for this project and the relevant brief lines. Do not copy a reference's text, assets or trademark presentation. If a reference was inaccessible, mark it untested and replace it before the critic relies on it.

Navigation model and findability: Specify navigation labels, grouping/order, destinations, current-location cues and back/escape behavior. Document discovery and movement between navigation levels. Specify search, filter and sort behavior when tasks require them; otherwise justify N/A. In Step 5, test finding priority destinations from realistic entry points using the proposed labels; record wrong turns and revisions. The Step 5 result is recorded as `navigation-findability`.

Exit with the complete sitemap, navigation model, task flows, section briefs and a single recommended direction. Reference selection must support the project's point of view, not impose a fashionable palette.

### 4. Grayscale desktop wireframes

Lay out the full desktop page/template set at 1440 CSS px using the real copy deck, clear reading order and numbered states. Explore two structural options for genuinely open sections unless the brief specifies otherwise; the separate three-to-five variant loop does not require duplicating every page. Keep all options grayscale and type-neutral at this phase.

Annotate column spans, reading order, grouping, content-driven height, intended media job, interaction/state behavior and brief references. Check the project's other permitted desktop widths. A centered section means its principal heading, copy and CTA are compositionally centered as a unit; centered images or cells alone do not count. Permit at most one such section per page. Do not evade the count by replacing semantic sections with anonymous containers.

At Step 4, check grayscale structure, copy and provenance and record issues for testing. The complete wireframe quality gate runs within the existing Step 5 gate (below), not here. No centered three-icon row, vague hero over three cards, arbitrary KPI row, icon soup or unsourced data survives structural review. A placeholder or missing decision is listed explicitly. Exit with the complete grayscale set and testable issues, not a polished home page alone.

### 5. Test and approve the desktop wireframes

Use the existing Step 5 gate as the explicit boundary before color and type work. No new approval gate is added. Test whether each audience can identify the purpose, find the relevant evidence and complete the primary task. Record tasks, actual participants or review method, observed failures and limitations; do not invent user research. The QA gate checks structure and accessibility intent. The writer checks copy and claim sources. The critic reviews structure and copy together against the brief and real reference work.

Before testing, define each priority task's neutral scenario, realistic starting point and observable completion criterion. Record participant/reviewer context, unaided completion, wrong turns, assistance and severity. Do not demonstrate the intended path first. Distinguish representative-user testing from expert walkthroughs and record remaining assumptions. Retest failed critical tasks after revisions against the new version. Resolve material uncertainty within the existing Step 5 approval gate. The record is `task-test`.

Within the existing Step 5 gate, after the task and navigation-findability tests and necessary retests, run the complete wireframe quality gate; require PASS before structure approval and Step 6. It checks structure, copy, provenance and placeholders, and its manual records carry the real Step 5 results (`task-test`, `navigation-findability`); it records unresolved visual checks (`accent-area` and `palette` always; `emphasis-order`, `type-measure`, `colour-roles` and `grid-per-width` when not yet judged in grayscale) as deferred to Step 6, never as passed. It is a run inside the existing gate, not a new approval gate. A wireframe PASS covers every declared desktop screen and locale. Each rendering must declare an existing page and receive the applicable structural/copy, accessibility, integrity and manual checks; missing coverage blocks structure approval. Each screen declares a default-state rendering per declared language (`screens[].states`), and the manual records cover every screen in every declared language.

Fix critical task failures and all structural/copy BLOCK findings, then obtain explicit approval for the complete scoped desktop wireframe set with version IDs. Approval of one page does not approve its siblings or templates. If unresolved copy or a consequential question prevents a meaningful structure decision, the gate stays On hold. This is where the no-AI-look checks run before styling; they run again on the final visual version.

### 6. Desktop visual design

Only after Step 5 approval, develop the chosen point of view with type, spacing, color, assets and motion. Start with the project's approved tokens and components. A new brand token still requires the existing approval. Pair display and reading roles deliberately; a single family with a justified role contrast is valid. Select fonts only after checking the project's language coverage, weight availability, licence and real text rendering.

Maintain one canonical specification for each reused component: anatomy, semantic variants, sizes, token bindings, content constraints and composition/usage rules. Map every screen instance to that specification and the existing component where available. Compare equivalent instances across screens; unexplained differences fail. The shared inventory is the handoff's `components` list (one definition per component: variants, states, token bindings and behaviour); every `screens[].components` entry references it by id. It is judged structurally in wireframes and fully at handoff, and recorded as `component-consistency`; the critic compares the instances across screens.

Use at most **six distinct rendered type sizes per page**: display, title, section heading, body, secondary text and label. Six supplies a useful hierarchy without arbitrary intermediate steps; fewer is preferable when sufficient. Document font family, fallback, size, line height, weight and tracking for each role. Weight and spacing carry distinctions before another size is added. Derive the sizes from a documented scale (a ratio or an explicit step rationale) and record the measure: body text runs about 45 to 75 characters per line at the reference width, body line height is at least 1.5 (a project rule), the WCAG 2.2 text-spacing overrides (line height 1.5, paragraph spacing 2, letter spacing 0.12 and word spacing 0.16 times the font size; SC 1.4.12) cause no loss of content or function, and long-form text never spans the full 1440 px. Check the scale in every required script, including numerals and mixed-direction strings; a script that needs a larger optical size gets it within the same ceiling. The six-size ceiling applies within each locale; translations may use a separately approved metric-equivalent scale with the same ceiling. Text must remain readable at user zoom. The scale, measure and line-height result is recorded as `type-measure`.

Use a named spacing scale and specify the grid, margins, gutters, alignment lines and section rhythm in tokens. For each width in `settings.desktopWidths`, specify the column count, gutter, outer margin, maximum content width and how sections recompose between widths (what stays aligned, what grows, where the measure caps). Column counts are positive unitless integers recorded beside the grid spec, not spacing tokens. Gutters, outer margins and maximum content widths reference approved dimension tokens with units (bare 0 allowed, no negative spacing tokens). Document recomposition between widths as behaviour. The result is recorded as `grid-per-width`. A layout that is only correct at 1440 px is incomplete. Every intentional grid break identifies the brief-backed reason and the reading-order consequence. Do not fabricate irregularity to appear human. Use neutrals and one strong accent. Define colour as named roles in the token JSON before use: page and raised surfaces, primary and secondary text, borders and dividers, the one accent, focus indicator and each semantic state (success, warning, error, information), each with its value and the job it serves. Build the contrast matrix at token level for every text-on-surface and non-text pair the screens use, including hover, focus, selected, error and disabled appearances (disabled text is exempt from the contrast minimum but must still be recognisable), and use only pairs that pass the WCAG 2.2 AA floor. A colour not in the role list does not appear on a screen. The role list and contrast matrix are recorded as `colour-roles`. The accent rule: accent-bearing area must be at most ten percent in both the full-page composition and the reviewed desktop viewport. Semantic colors may communicate states but require text or shape as well; they are not extra decorative accents. The screenshot review catches color inside media and effects that DOM measurements miss.

Before styling and again on the visual version, record each screen's intended emphasis order (the first, second and third thing a visitor should read) and its single primary action per viewport; at most one element per viewport carries the strongest emphasis. Verify the order on a 1440 px screenshot viewed small and blurred (and in grayscale at Step 4/5); when the visible order differs from the intended one, change size, weight, position, contrast or space, not the copy. Record the result in the `craft` artifact and as `emphasis-order`.

Avoid a default purple-blue combination, generic gradient hero, stock glass effects or repeated rounded-card grid. An existing brand conflict is reported for a decision, not silently rebranded. Each visual and icon must help someone understand, navigate, decide or recognize something. Remove it when that job cannot be named.

For each motion, record the job, trigger, duration in milliseconds, easing, affected elements, interruption/cleanup behavior and static fallback. Under reduced motion there is no movement. Essential content remains visible with JavaScript disabled; the project's specified script-failure deadline is an acceptance check. Motion has no value merely because a page feels quiet without it.

Render every required language and direction with actual writer-approved copy, then perform a clearly labelled synthetic long-text stress test at 150 percent of the slot's typical length. This synthetic text is test input, never publishable copy. Character ranges are planning guidance: if an approved translation exceeds one, revise the range and layout rather than truncate. Use logical start/end layout rules; check mixed numbers, punctuation, links and directional icons in RTL. Never mirror logos or text blindly. Exit with approved desktop visuals, complete state behavior and no blocking craft, copy or accessibility defects.

## Threat model for the pre-handoff gate

Pages are authored by Fannan or a human designer: honest but possibly sloppy, not an attacker. The gate must catch honest mistakes and ordinary authoring patterns completely (body or html clipping, zero-size or `display: contents` wrappers, open shadow roots, frames, objects and embeds, stylesheets loaded from `data:` or `blob:` URLs, state pages), and must fail closed on anything it cannot measure (closed shadow roots, unreadable or unserved stylesheets, frames, a vector field or native-function check that could not run, JavaScript-off content that is not equivalent).

Deliberate obfuscation is outside the main threat model: for example JavaScript string escapes, comment tricks inside strings, patching `Function.prototype`, or base64-encoded stylesheets. Cheap defences are applied where they are simple (escapes are decoded and raw text is matched with and without comment stripping; the reduced-motion counter binds the native `requestAnimationFrame` at install and `call`/`apply`/`bind`/`Reflect.apply` are in the native-function check; `data:`/`blob:` stylesheets are refused). Anything beyond that is listed in `UNTESTED.md` as a known limit and is raised as ENH, not P0-P2, in future rounds. Defence in depth stays: computed-style checks, the JavaScript-off pass and the source scan.

## Measurement contract

The reference viewport is 1440 × 900 CSS pixels at device scale one, with a full-page screenshot as well. Visit every declared screen and applicable state. Wait for local fonts and assets; log failed or blocked loads. Keep external requests, service workers and WebSockets blocked during the local check. Capture file hashes and the version under test. A reference PNG is 1440 pixels wide and named `screen--state--locale--vNN.png`; its hash identifies the version. A screenshot from another revision cannot support a PASS. The checker also renders every declared `settings.desktopWidths` width and 320 CSS px (the 400% reflow equivalent of a 1280 px desktop) and fails horizontal overflow, text that actually overflows a hidden or clipped box (ellipsis styling alone is not a failure; `body` is screened too, so `overflow-x: hidden` or `clip` on `html` and `body` cannot hide a wide layout), text or media pushed past the left edge (except inside a horizontal scroller, visually hidden sr-only content of at most 1×1 px or `clip: rect(0 0 0 0)` / `clip-path: inset(50%)`, and a focusable off-screen skip link that appears on focus) and a language/direction mismatch; this is a screen, not mobile approval, and manual zoom and text-spacing evidence stays required.

Mark visible text wrappers with `data-copy-slot="ID"` and match every displayed string to the approved locale variant in the copy deck, including interface and error labels. Unlisted or mismatched text blocks final handoff. Declare semantic section, component, copy-slot and media IDs in the reference DOM and match them to the handoff inventory. The critic inspects the full screenshot and semantic outline so unmarked containers cannot evade a count. CSS/computed-style counts are recorded as measurements. Layout intent, claims, tasteful restraint and the purpose of an image remain reviewed judgments with an artifact, reviewer, explanation and brief line references. A guessed PASS flag is not evidence.

Where the browser test uses a proxy, label it as a proxy in `evidence.json`. In particular, DOM element rectangles do not measure the actual colored pixels of text, images, gradients or overlapping regions. For accent coverage, the documented acceptance measure is visible accent area divided by total rendered area, separately for the full page and each reviewed viewport. The reviewer supplies an annotated color mask or measured screenshot regions for image/canvas/SVG content and any disputed DOM estimate. Use union area, not summed overlapping boxes. Borderline or unmeasurable coverage remains FAIL pending evidence; do not round 10.1% down to 10%. The automated accent screen fails closed: if a declared accent token (`--accent`, `--colors-accent`, `--colors-primary`) is not a valid colour, or no accent token is declared, the ratios are null and the handoff check fails; it is never treated as zero accent.

The automated probes and axe run in a separate browser world (`tools/probe-world.mjs`, a CDP isolated world; Chromium only): they see the page's DOM and CSS but not its JavaScript globals, so a page script cannot patch `getComputedStyle`, `querySelectorAll` or `axe` to hide a failure. `tools/integrity.mjs` cross-checks the positional page: the same probes in the page's own world (a difference is a tampering sign), the page world's probe functions (they must still be native), and a JavaScript-off pass. More glass or a failing 320 px screen with JavaScript off fails as `integrity mismatch` unless the handoff's `jsOffDifferences` records the expected difference with a reason. Text contrast is cross-checked with JavaScript off too: CSS opacity and `filter: opacity()` on the text and its ancestors are composited; any other filter, a blend mode, a backdrop filter or a mask on the chain cannot be resolved, so that text is counted in `contrastEffects` and the cross-check is INCOMPLETE, never a silent pass (the glass hard fail is unchanged). Native control text (input and textarea values or placeholders, button-type input labels, a select's chosen option) is contrast-checked, and the content comparison records each control, link, image and labelled element with its accessible name and displayed value, so a label, value or `aria-label` that only script supplies makes the JavaScript-off content differ.

Unresolved compositing on displayed text in either rendering blocks handoff as INCOMPLETE. Token contrast proofs and `jsOffDifferences` cannot waive it. Retaining the effect requires a reviewed measurement implementation that resolves its actual composition for every applicable rendering. Backdrop-filter remains a hard FAIL.

Rendered-visibility backstop: the CSS contrast computation is the first pass; what the browser actually painted is the backstop, in both renderings (JavaScript on and off). Pixels decide visibility, not AA: the backstop asks whether the text is actually painted, uncovered and readable at all; AA (4.5:1, 3:1 for large text) stays with the CSS pass whenever the CSS pass resolves the colours, and with axe and the manual WCAG review. Targets are every rendered text node (display:contents wrappers included), every native control showing text (value, placeholder or chosen option) and every named element without painted text of its own (accessible names only, hit test only). Text is measured line by line: each text node's word ranges are grouped into line boxes, so one hidden line of a paragraph is found. The scan is directed at targets, not fixed bands: after a first look at the top of the page, every line not yet measured is scrolled into view (its scrollable ancestors, such as an app shell's main panel or a tab strip, first; then the document) and measured once finite animations have finished and the painted state of every element (opacity, transform, filter, clip-path, colours, position) is unchanged about 100 ms apart, so CSS and script-driven entrances and scroll reveals are measured at rest (bounded at 2 s per step; elements under infinite animations are left out). The page bottom is then collected again, and so is the end of every inner scrollport (an app shell's panel), so content added while scrolling is found, for at most four passes; a scan that stops at its step bound (300) or still finds new content after the last pass is INCOMPLETE. (a) A hit test at three points per line checks that the text, its own subtree or an ancestor is on top. An opaque cover (a replaced element other than an SVG, or a background colour at 90% or more opacity) at more than half the points obscures the line; a translucent cover (an SVG, which is mostly transparent shapes, a lighter background colour, a background image or gradient, a backdrop filter, other text) is compositing the CSS pass cannot see, so that line is graded by its pixels. Layers a hit test cannot see are possible covers too: a positioned `::before` or `::after` that paints, on the text's element or an ancestor (a loading veil, a scrim), and a positioned `pointer-events:none` layer that paints; a line under one at more than half the points is graded by its pixels at 3:1. Layers behind the text (negative z-index) are not covers. A gradient pseudo-element is the fade of a truncated block (a "read more" clamp) only when its ancestor clips text that actually overflows and the gradient sits at the edge where the text is cut; the lines under it are then cut text, not displayed, like a clipped line (the width screen still fails a partly clipped block, as the clipping rule below requires). Any other gradient pseudo-element, such as a veil over a card whose text fits, is a cover graded by its pixels. On a declared state other than `default`, the top layer is respected: with a modal dialog open (or a shown element with `aria-modal="true"`) the rest of the page is inert and is counted as not displayed, page text an open popover covers is not graded, and the dialog's and the popover's own text are graded; a non-modal open dialog is not inert, so text it covers stays graded and is obscured. (b) Pixels: from a viewport screenshot after the page settles, over the middle 60% of the line box, the background is the modal colour, ink pixels differ from it by more than 24 in a channel, the readability ratio is the 90th percentile of the ink pixels' contrast against the background and the peak is the strongest ink pixel. Where the CSS pass resolved the colours and no translucent layer covers the line, the line fails only when it is effectively not painted: fewer than three ink pixels or a peak under 1.5:1 (colours that resolve to at least 3:1 but paint under 1.5:1 are hidden by an effect the CSS pass cannot see; light and thin weights that pass AA by colour are left to the CSS pass). Where the CSS pass could not resolve the colours, the pixels grade readability: under 3:1 is unreadable (under 1.5:1 for a line with fewer than 30 ink pixels, such as a single thin glyph). Rendered text under 4 px high is unreadable. Text that is obscured or unreadable with JavaScript on fails; text that is readable with JavaScript on but obscured, unreadable, unmeasurable or hidden with it off is an integrity mismatch, and a reviewed `jsOffDifferences` entry for it never makes it PASS. Text clipped to nothing by an ancestor that cannot be scrolled (an inactive carousel slide, an accordion collapsed with max-height:0), the same in both renderings, is not displayed: it is counted (`textHidden`) like display:none and graded through the declared states that show it. That text counts as not displayed only when a declared state shows it, which the width screen enforces (below). Text the backstop cannot measure (no box, outside the document, too few pixels, not reachable by scrolling, not reached before the bound) is INCOMPLETE, never PASS. Hide inactive content with `hidden`, `display:none` or `visibility:hidden`; visually hidden assistive text (the same sr-only rule as the width screen), an off-screen skip link that appears on focus, and the text of a disabled control (no contrast requirement in WCAG 1.4.3) are counted, not graded. Native authored strings (button-type input values, placeholders, option labels and accessible names) are copy: they are matched to the copy deck and screened for placeholders and filler like visible text, and a control's displayed value and its accessible name are compared separately with JavaScript off. The copy deck approves the displayed text and the accessible name separately (a slot's optional `ariaLabel`); a name identical to the displayed value is one string. A name taken by `aria-labelledby` from a hidden or undisplayed element is a name to approve the same way, and an approved `ariaLabel` must appear in its cited source like the text.

Automated contrast incomplete results: axe reports `color-contrast` as incomplete when it cannot resolve the colours (text over a photo, a gradient or a scrim). Text over images is graded in three zones on the faintest real glyph cluster, and only for an element the backstop measured (the same composed path) with every line uncovered, in BOTH renderings: (a) clearly at or above AA (5% above 4.5:1, or above 3:1 for large text) passes; (b) clearly below 3:1 (5% under it) FAILS, and no review can override it; (c) anything between, or a faintest glyph that could not be measured in either rendering (when the other rendering measured below 3:1, (b) applies), is INCOMPLETE, and is resolved only by a PASS `wcag-review` record for the same screen, language and state that cites the screenshot (its artifact is that state's approved frame or the checker's screenshot, or its note names one of them) AND names the worst region checked in `worstRegion`. A text-shadow or halo counts as the text's background (WCAG 1.4.3), so text is measured against the halo where it surrounds the glyph. The faintest-text measure never drops a faint glyph. It captures the element's text box four times: as painted; with the text made transparent but its text-shadow kept (the local background under every glyph pixel, the halo included); and with the text forced to solid black and to solid white, with no shadow and opacity 1 on the element and every ancestor. Glyph coverage is the difference between the black and white captures, so it is geometric: it depends neither on the text's own contrast nor on opacity, and a caption at 70% opacity is measured, not refused. In every glyph-core pixel (coverage at least 75%; the anti-aliased edge is excluded) the composited text colour is recovered and graded against its own local background. Core pixels are grouped into connected clusters (a glyph or touching glyphs), each cluster counts its median pixel, and the element's ratio is the MINIMUM over all clusters: one word over the light part of a photo (white on #c8c8c8, 1.67:1) fails the whole element even when the rest of the line sits on a dark area. Letter clusters are never dropped on a background that is not solid; thin letters (the stems of i and l) are kept at every size; only punctuation-size clusters, at most a quarter of the font size in both directions, over a solid background are left out, and only while larger clusters remain. A high percentile of all ink is never used for AA. Neither is a percentile of the clusters. The resolution is recorded per element (`contrastNodes`, with its `zone`); any other incomplete rule, and any element that fails one condition, stays blocking. One exception covers the JavaScript-off rendering only: a declared non-default state reachable only by script, declared `requiresScript: true` with its trigger recorded, resolves on the scripted rendering alone (same element, AA and review conditions); the default state always needs both renderings, and a `requiresScript` claim on a state that renders with JavaScript off (every text the scripted rendering shows is shown without script, compared by text rather than element position) fails as an integrity mismatch. For a valid claim the JavaScript-off baseline is the default state's JavaScript-off rendering, and content present only in the scripted state passes only as approved copy slots placed in that state. A script-only state may replace a default slot (search results replacing the page description) only when the copy deck places the new slot in that state and does not place the old one there; a replaced slot that is still placed in the state, and any change to copy both renderings show, stay INCOMPLETE. The `requiresScript` check compares counted word tokens, so splitting a text run into inline elements does not change it.

ENH backlog: a future exception for an unresolved compositing effect is acceptable only with a pixel-measured proof per rendering (screenshot text-pixel contrast with the effect applied, a named approver and the evidence hash), never with token pairs.

Clipping is one rule based on states and widths, whatever the CSS pattern: it applies to text and controls only. Cropped media is never clipped content: an image cropped by its frame, an image centred in a `picture`, a hover zoom, a round avatar crop, a cropped video, a decorative SVG or shape that bleeds out of its section, `object-fit` and background images all pass; text or a control inside a cropped media wrapper is still checked, and a box that cuts nothing (or only media and decoration) is not a clip. Content Chrome does not render is not on screen: the content of a closed `<details>` (a mobile menu), `display:none`, `content-visibility: hidden` and closed popovers are not measured, and their open state is graded only when it is declared. The fallback content inside `video`, `audio`, `object` and `canvas` is not rendered either, so it is not text on screen (the element itself is media, or a nested document). Links and named controls are content in their own right, by their box, their rendered label text and their accessible name (every WAI-ARIA widget role, such as slider, spinbutton, listbox, option, treeitem, combobox, switch, tab, menuitemcheckbox and menuitemradio, and the native equivalents: links, buttons, inputs, `select`, `textarea`, `summary`, `progress`, `meter`; a label cut by the control's box or a wrapper is clipped text, never a media crop, and a wrapper is a media crop only if everything it cuts is media or decoration): an icon-only link (`aria-label`, or an image's alt text), a checkbox named by `aria-labelledby`, a button. Content is matched by a typed print: text by its text (`text:`), a control by its role and accessible name (`button:Subscribe`, `link:Your profile`); text matches only text, so a heading with the same words never stands in for a lost button. Text inside a control belongs to the control. A control at a narrower width matches its 1440 control only by the SAME accessible name, one to one: each lost control needs its own match, so 'Save as' hidden next to 'Save', and a second Remove button hidden next to the first, are lost. The roles of one action family (button, link, menuitem, menuitemcheckbox, menuitemradio) are interchangeable, so 'Export report' moved into a declared More menu as a menu item passes. A label that shortens visually ("Demo" for "Request a demo") needs an `aria-label` keeping the full name, or it is lost; a link left with no name (a wordmark whose text is hidden) is lost. Footer copies of the desktop nav links do not stand in for the hidden nav links: a mobile menu needs its `menu-open` state declared. (1) Any text or control shown at 1440 in a state must be fully shown, or reachable by scrolling, at every declared width, 320 included, in that state, or it fails (a table whose columns an `overflow:hidden` wrapper cuts at 320, nav links cut by a nowrap bar); text or a control shown at 1440 but not shown at a width (`display:none` below a breakpoint: a hidden Subscribe button or terms line) fails there too (WCAG 1.4.10, no loss of content or function) unless the same text or accessible name is reachable at that width, in the same state (a footer link) or in a declared state at that width (`menu-open` for a mobile menu); matching is by text or name, never by position; (2) content clipped to nothing in a state at 1440 (a collapsed accordion panel, the other slides of a carousel) counts as not displayed only if another declared state of the same screen and language shows it at 1440 (the accordion's expanded state, each slide's state), and that state is then graded normally; (3) content clipped in every declared state (a fixed-height card whose last paragraph lies below the edge), or partly clipped anywhere (a partly cut accordion, a marquee), fails. A read-more clamp is the one partial cut that can pass: intentional truncation (`line-clamp`, or `text-overflow: ellipsis` on the cut edge) passes only when one declared state shows ALL the clamped content at that width (every text the clamped box cuts, wholly or partly: a second paragraph hidden by the clamp too), and fails otherwise; a clamp may only hide text: a control the clamp cuts, wholly or partly, is never excused (partly clipped), even when an expanded state shows it; a fixed-height box that cuts text without a clamp fails even with an expanded state. Reveals and truncations are matched to the revealing state by text, not by DOM position. Designers must declare reveal states: every expanded panel, slide or other content hidden by a box that cannot scroll needs a declared state, with its own page, that shows it, or the handoff is blocked. In the wireframe phase, where only default states are rendered, an unresolved reveal is DEFERRED. `evidence.json` records each clip with its `rule` (`reveal`, `truncated`, `partly clipped`, `shown at 1440, lost at this width` or `clip`), the cut elements (`whole`, `partial`) and the state that reveals them (`revealedBy`), each width's `lost` text with the state where it is reachable (`reachableIn`), and media crops (`mediaCrops`, information only).

R24 rules for controls at narrow widths. A control's label text is always measured, whatever its border: a borderless button whose label is cut by its own box is clipped, never a media crop. Matches to a reveal or expanded state are counted one to one, like losses between widths: two hidden Downloads need two shown. The only role swaps allowed are a tablist adapted into an accordion (disclosure buttons, a `summary` or a button with `aria-expanded`, with the same names) and a menubar adapted into a menu in a declared state; the container counts as covered only when every item is matched one to one by name. R25 adds exactly one more swap: content tabs becoming headed sections, covered only when every tab gets one shown heading with the same accessible name at that width and every panel's content and actions are still shown. No other role swap is accepted. A hidden control counts once, as one name per owning control (text nested inside a button or link is part of it), and the one-to-one count is shared across all collapsed panels of one screen, state and width; carousel slide states keep their own counts. A declared state rescues content collapsed to zero visible area (max-height or height 0 with overflow hidden) exactly as it rescues display:none; a partial cut still fails. R26 replaces name counting with element identity: every control and text item shown at 1440 is tracked as that element, across widths and declared states. At a narrow width an item counts as shown only if the same element is shown in the default or a declared state, or, through the closed swap list alone (the action family, tabs to an accordion, tabs to headed sections, a menubar to a menu), by a distinct element with the same accessible name that was not itself shown at 1440. Each covering element covers one item only, so footer copies never stand in for nav links and a declared menu-open state must really open the menu. Tabs becoming headed sections need every tab panel declared as a state, and the narrow width must show each action as many times as the panels do in total (R27: at least that many, counted inside the tablist's sections); a panel's own heading is never subtracted. Carousel slides are tracked by element, so slides may repeat a button name. Moving content is settled before each width is measured: finite animations and transitions are finished and recorded as motion at capture; infinite animations are left to the marquee rule. Collapsed pagination (1, 2, …, 10 with Previous and Next) still fails at 320: let the page links wrap.

R27 makes element identity a stable key, not position: an element is its id when it has one, otherwise its nearest landmark (a tab panel counts as its own scope) plus its role family, accessible name, link target and order among same-key elements in that landmark, so a menu-open state that adds a Close button before the links still matches them, while footer copies, in another landmark, never do. Identity keeps across role changes within the action family (button, link, menuitem, menuitemcheckbox, menuitemradio), so a toolbar whose buttons become menu items at 320 is the same controls. Anything the source state already shows at that width, such as a footer link, is set aside before name matching and never covers a hidden item, so a carousel with an undeclared slide fails even with a footer Shop now. Tabs becoming headed sections need at least each panel's actions at the narrow width, counted only inside that tablist's sections; unrelated buttons elsewhere, such as a sticky mobile Download, count neither way. Besides the same element and the closed swap list, two extra coverage rules are approved: a distinct element not shown at 1440 with the same role and accessible name, and a distinct element with the same text. Each still covers one item only.
At the 320 px reflow width, ordinary text or media overflowing any sideways scroller, to the left or the right, fails. A `table`, `pre`, `code`, `role="grid"`/`role="table"` with real rows, or an element marked `data-fannan-2d="reason"` is only a **candidate**, recorded in `evidence.json` with its element and reason and never excused automatically: its scroller must fit inside 320 px and be keyboard-usable, the text around it must reflow, resizable media is not a candidate, and every candidate needs a `reflowExceptions` decision in the handoff, or the final handoff is blocked. There is no cap on the number of candidates.

The accent must be the approved palette token (`colors.accent`, else `colors.primary`, from the hash-checked tokens). At final handoff an invalid or missing accent variable, or one that differs from the approved token, fails with a message naming the variable; the wireframe phase records the accent as DEFERRED, never PASS. The accent area is a DOM background-area proxy and is approximate; the screenshot review decides. The automated check does not measure accent area or coverage exactly and does not fail other off-palette colours painted on the page: accent area, coverage and stray colours are decided by the screenshots and the manual `accent-area` and `palette` reviews (panel A; R12 M19 closed on that basis). The accent does not have to appear on a control.

The spacing-scale probe checks all four margins (logical margins included; `auto` and 0 are ignored), padding and gaps. An element marked `data-fannan-spacing-exception="reason"` is recorded with its element, id, reason, values and screen/state/lang in the handoff evidence (`checks.spacingExceptions`). At final handoff it excuses its values only when the handoff has a matching `spacingExceptions` entry for that element id and screen/state/lang, with a reason and the approver as reviewer; otherwise the handoff is blocked (wireframes record it as DEFERRED). A blank reason, or an element without an id, excuses nothing. Margins on `html` and `body` are screened too.

Negative spacing **tokens** are banned: the approved scale holds only positive lengths, so a negative value is always off-scale. An approved margin exception (above, with the approver named) may still excuse a negative margin on one element; that is a reviewed per-element decision, not a token (R13 D1 closed on this basis).

Frames are never excused: there is no `frameExceptions` entry. axe and the design probes run in the main document only, so any `iframe`, `frame`, `object`, `embed` or `fencedframe` fails the final handoff (DEFERRED in wireframes). Where the design needs embedded third-party content (a map, a video, a booking widget), the handoff uses a **static poster** image in its place plus an **engineering note** in `HANDOFF.md` naming the embed, its source and where the engineer inserts it. In-frame scanning is revisited only once every gate runs inside frames (R13 q1).

## Manual evidence that the checker requires

`manualChecks` includes `point-of-view`, `brief-trace`, `generic-hero` (marketing and landing screens only), `decoration-job`, `card-grid`, `dashboard-tropes`, `rule-of-three`, `reference-work`, `craft`, `wcag-review`, `accent-area`, `palette`, `rules-copy`, `language-overflow`, `discovery-evidence` (the Step 1 discovery ledger), `navigation-findability` (Step 3 navigation model and its Step 5 findability test), `interaction-states` (reachable states beyond the seven), `form-behavior` (forms and consequential actions, or a justified N/A in the note), `task-test` (Step 5 neutral task testing and retests), `feedback-log` (the approver's feedback log), `emphasis-order` (the emphasis order and blurred-screenshot check), `type-measure` (the type scale with measure, line height and the SC 1.4.12 text-spacing check), `colour-roles` (the colour roles and token-level contrast matrix) `grid-per-width` (the grid for every declared desktop width) and `component-consistency` (each reused component matches its one shared definition on every screen; judged structurally in wireframes and fully at handoff). The `craft` artifact may hold the last four; each still needs its own record. In the wireframe phase `accent-area` and `palette` are DEFERRED, and `emphasis-order`, `type-measure`, `colour-roles` and `grid-per-width` are PASS or DEFERRED; every other ID needs PASS in both phases.

The feedback-log artifact records each item's exact words, scope, version, interpretation, decision, resulting change and confirmation. A deferral needs the approver's recorded disposition. Missing or unresolved items fail. If no feedback was received, record that explicitly with reviewer and scope. Every record identifies its screen, language and state, PASS/FAIL, reviewer, artifact path, brief line references and a concrete note. An artifact may cover several states, but each record identifies the part supporting its exact scope. The artifact contains the actual rubric/check result, screenshot references and measured values where relevant. Missing coverage for a screen is a failure even if another screen passed. A grayscale artifact records visual tests as deferred while judging structural craft; it does not sign off final color or typography.

On every screen type (the `copy-truth` row; on `marketing` and `landing` screens also `generic-hero`), the critic asks: with the company name masked, does the headline still express a precise offering, audience task or independently sourced distinction from this brief? Record the compared alternative categories and the reason. If it could be pasted unchanged onto arbitrary company sites, fail and return the slot to the writer. Do not replace an approved headline without the existing approval process.

For functional dashboards, derive content from the user's decisions and workflows. Use a ranked queue, trend or table when that is what the task requires; do not add a summary-card row merely because dashboards often have one. Every number requires a real source, scope and update meaning. Static sample data must be explicitly labelled as a fixture and cannot enter a real handoff as production data. “Live” requires a real documented data path; animation is not evidence.

## Accessibility evidence, accurately scoped

Record each text foreground/background pair and state. Normal text needs 4.5:1 contrast; large text needs 3:1 using WCAG's definition of large text. Contrast uses specified/computed colors, not antialiased screenshot edges. Do not round a failing ratio upward. [W3C contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).

Applicable control boundaries, meaningful icons and graphical objects need 3:1 against adjacent colors. Record their actual role and applicable exceptions rather than claiming every decorative border must pass. Status also has a readable label or shape. [W3C non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).

Record sequential keyboard focus, visible focus before/after, modal entry/escape/return, traps and sticky overlays. AA focus-not-obscured requires that the focused control is not entirely hidden by author content; prefer full visibility as this project's stricter usability target. Focus Appearance 2.4.13 is AAA, so do not mislabel it as the AA criterion. [W3C focus-not-obscured](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum).

Measure actual pointer targets. A 24 × 24 CSS-pixel target is the baseline; any smaller target needs a documented qualifying spacing, equivalent-control, inline, user-agent or essential exception. Bounding boxes and an “inline” tag are only screening heuristics. [W3C target-size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum).

Test non-dragging alternatives for dragging operations, and accessible authentication without an unsupported cognitive test where authentication exists. If absent, record N/A with the screen inventory that proves absence. [W3C WCAG 2.2 additions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/).

Check 200% text resizing and 400% desktop zoom/reflow (equivalent 320 CSS-pixel width), including applicable two-dimensional content exceptions. This is accessibility validation, not authorization to design phone layouts. Document browser zoom and viewport used. [W3C reflow guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html).

Set the page language, mark language changes and specify base direction separately. Test real RTL content, mixed-direction identifiers, punctuation and numbers; use appropriate directional markup rather than reversing strings. [W3C internationalization guidance](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-html-dir).

# Critic rubric — copy and design together

The critic is independent and read-only. Load the frozen brief, rules, settings, copy/fact ledgers, reference board, scoped screen/state renders and integrated quality evidence. Do not start from the designer's self-assessment. Evaluate each page and all scoped states/locales against their real reference work; check a composition both at full-page scale and at readable 1440-pixel size. Missing evidence is FAIL, not benefit of the doubt.

## Score anchors and pass rule

Use zero-to-four scores only to make iteration visible: **0** absent or contradicted; **1** recognizable intent with major defects; **2** functional but generic or materially incomplete; **3** clear, specific and supported with all required checks passing; **4** unusually resolved craft, supported by concrete comparisons and detail. A score of four needs an explanation, not enthusiasm.

Every row and every page receives a public **PASS or FAIL**, a score, an artifact reference and a reason. PASS requires at least 3 in every applicable row, all hard checks passing and no open P0/P1. Scores are never averaged to compensate for a failed dimension. Each row has an ID (in brackets in the table). In the handoff record every critic copy or design check names its row in `row`, and the final round carries at least one passing, evidenced check for each of the eight rows; a missing row fails the contract. A WARN can remain only with a named owner and explicit disposition and no unanswered build question.

| Dimension | PASS at 3 requires | Evidence / automatic failure |
|---|---|---|
| Brief and point of view (`brief-point-of-view`) | One legible idea serving this audience/job, expressed consistently across the page | Brief lines for every section and deliberate decision; any orphan section fails |
| Copy specificity and truth (`copy-truth`) | Accurate approved wording, identifiable offering/task and confirmed proof, on every screen type | Slot/source/claim ledger, project-wording checks; transferable headline, invented proof or filler fails on any screen, `functional` included (`generic-hero` adds the swap test on `marketing` and `landing` screens) |
| Content hierarchy and task flow (`hierarchy-task-flow`) | Reading order, emphasis and CTA help the named task; first impression matches actual content; the recorded emphasis order holds on the blurred screenshot | Task walkthrough, neutral task-test record and annotated frame; vague hero over icon cards or misleading primary action fails |
| Typography and language (`typography-language`) | Deliberate role pairing, at most six sizes from a documented scale, a body measure of about 45 to 75 characters, body line height of at least 1.5, no loss under the WCAG text-spacing overrides, correct scripts/directions and uncut translations | Token-to-DOM mapping, language renders, long-text/zoom checks; clipping or missing script support fails |
| Spacing, rhythm and grid (`spacing-rhythm-grid`) | Repeatable spacing roles with purposeful contrast in density; columns, gutters, margins and maximum width specified for every declared desktop width; any grid break aids the brief | Alignment/grid overlay and section-to-section comparison; random spacing/novelty fails |
| Color, imagery and restraint (`colour-imagery-restraint`) | Named colour roles with a token-level contrast matrix, legible neutral base, one accent within area budget, useful licensed imagery, no default effects | Measured color roles, contrast, screenshot, asset jobs; palette/card/gradient/glass rule violation fails |
| Interaction, states and motion (`interaction-states-motion`) | Every applicable state and recovery path clear, including reachable states beyond the seven and form behavior; motion explains change and has static fallback | State matrix, keyboard story, reduced-motion/no-JS proof; an unspecified reachable state, transition or applicable form behavior fails, as do fake live behavior and color-only status |
| Accessibility and handoff precision (`accessibility-handoff`) | Applicable WCAG 2.2 AA criteria supported and each implementation choice specified | Automated plus manual evidence and canonical checklist; missing evidence, open question or unmet AA fails |

For a wireframe review, typography means hierarchy/readability, color means grayscale restraint, and interaction means state intent. Record color/font/motion polish as **deferred to Step 6**, outside that phase's score. Do not reward visual polish before structure is approved. The handoff rubric covers all rows without those deferrals.

## Cross-screen component comparison

In every round, compare the equivalent instances of each reused component across all screens in scope against its shared definition: anatomy, variant, size, token bindings, content constraints, states and usage. Record the screens compared, each difference with its reason or defect, and a PASS or FAIL in the final round's `componentComparison`. An unexplained difference fails `component-consistency` and the relevant rubric row.

## Specificity test and reference comparison

The swap test applies to promotional hero copy and claims of distinction, on screens of type `marketing` or `landing`. For each such page, quote its actual hero line from the approved copy deck, mask the brand name and compare its meaning with two unrelated business categories. If both could use it unchanged, mark `generic-hero` FAIL. A factual descriptor, named audience job or specific proposition must make the line belong to this brief. Do not invent competitor quotations. The writer owns revisions and existing approval still applies. A `functional` screen (settings, a dashboard, a form or another task screen) has no promotional hero: it uses a clear task heading such as "Settings", and `generic-hero` is not required for it.

Compare each page with the three inspected reference works selected in Step 3. State the exact aspect being compared, the reference evidence, where this design falls short and how the brief justifies a deliberate difference. “Looks premium” or “feels human” is not a criterion. No reference URL may be presented as inspected unless it was opened and reviewed. The current candidate contains a rubric, not a fabricated comparison against an unbuilt project.

## Defects and the bounded loop

P0 means a serious truth, safety, access or essential task failure; P1 means a broken required rule, missing critical evidence or a build-blocking omission. P2/P3 are lesser issues only when they do not violate a BLOCK check. A required check cannot be downgraded to make a page pass.

At each round record screen/state hashes, every score and PASS/FAIL, defects with severity, evidence, owner and next correction. The designer revises; the critic evaluates the new artifact independently. Stop at three rounds maximum, or earlier when total improvement is less than one point between rounds. The stop packet lists all round scores, what changed, open defects and the decision needed from the approver. It does not authorize handoff, a fourth round or relaxed thresholds. An approval cannot waive the WCAG floor, factual truth or security rules.

The final verdict is either **PASS for the explicitly listed scope/version** or **FAIL — return to the relevant step**. A draft, an unavailable reference or a capped loop is never “pass with conditions” for Step 9.
